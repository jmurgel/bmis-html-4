Story: Maya's Lemonade Stand

One week, five transactions, and a notebook that always balances.

Home See Practice Story Listen

Monday: The piggy bank

Maya breaks open her piggy bank and puts $50 into a cash box labeled "Maya's Lemonade." Her grandpa Ledger, a retired accountant, hands her a notebook. "Every time money moves," he says, "write down what came in and where it came from. Both sides, every time."

She writes: Cash +$50 = Maya's Equity +$50. "It's all mine," she grins. "That's what equity means," says Grandpa.

Tuesday: Borrowing from Leo

Her brother Leo lends her $20 for a bigger pitcher, as long as she pays him back. Maya's cash grows, but now she owes someone.

Cash +$20 = Owed to Leo +$20. "That's a liability," Grandpa says. "Money you have, but it's not all yours."

Wednesday: Shopping day

She spends $30 on lemons, sugar and cups. Her cash shrinks, but she's not poorer: she now owns supplies.

Supplies +$30, Cash −$30. "You traded one asset for another," Grandpa says. "Your total didn't change."

Thursday: The big sale

A soccer team stops by and buys $60 of lemonade. She uses up $20 of supplies doing it.

Cash +$60 → Revenue +$60.
Supplies −$20 → Expense −$20.

"Revenue makes your equity grow," says Grandpa, "and expenses shrink it. What's left over, $40, is your net income."

Friday: Paying Leo back

Maya hands Leo his $20. Cash −$20, Owed to Leo −$20.

She adds up her notebook:

Cash$80
Supplies$10
Total assets$90
Liabilities$0
Equity ($50 invested + $40 net income)$90
Liabilities + equity$90

"It balances!" Maya shouts. Grandpa smiles. "It always will, if you write down both sides."

What Maya learned

  • Assets = Liabilities + Equity, after every transaction.
  • Borrowing increases assets and liabilities.
  • Buying supplies with cash is a swap. Totals don't change.
  • Revenue − Expenses = Net income, which grows equity.
